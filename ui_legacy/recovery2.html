AXIOM Recover · what the metric was hiding

The measure decides the winner

Same batch, same gates, same frozen customer model — scored two ways. Counted per record, the smart policy wins. Counted per rupee, it loses to a blind retry loop. The lines cross because 20% of the records hold 93% of the money, and only one of these two numbers can see that.

What the original run did not charge

gates.js prices a human review and writes it into the audit chain. recover.js never adds it to the spend tracker, because ESCALATE_HUMAN sits outside its acting set. The smart policy escalates far more often than the baseline, so the labour goes missing unevenly — and the published delta absorbs the difference.

Where the money actually is

Records ordered by size. Everything left of the dashed line sits at or above the ₹10,000 approval ceiling — which the original loop routed to a human and then marked resolved forever, recovering nothing.

Corrected run · seed batch

Escalation labour billed to whichever arm requested it. Ceiling escalations routed to a review queue that returns approved records to automation instead of abandoning them. The queue is offered to every arm, so it is not a privilege the new policy gets.

Is the difference real

Paired 95% bootstrap intervals on the per-seed difference against the baseline. Every arm faces an identical population on each seed, so the difference is paired and the paired spread is the honest thing to quote.

What the agent changed its mind about

Beliefs come from agent-priors.json, never from the frozen truth table — a policy that read the answer key would score beautifully and mean nothing. Two of these priors were set deliberately wrong to see whether the bandit would find them.

The opt-out objection is a price, not a flaw

The policy's appetite for voice calls falls out of one number: what a churned customer is assumed to cost. The base rate sets it at ₹480 and sources it honestly as a business decision rather than a benchmark. Raise it and the policy restrains itself — no new rule, no cap.
